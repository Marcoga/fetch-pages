🧗 Boulder Blueprint

3-Week Daily Progression Plan — from 5abc to crushing 6s

⚡ Quick Rules

  • Warm up every time — 10 min easy traversing + finger/wrist stretches. Non-negotiable.
  • Climb with your feet, not your arms — the #1 beginner mistake is pulling with arms. Push with legs.
  • Rest between attempts — 2-3 min between hard sends. Bouldering is power, not endurance.
  • Stop before failure — end session when moves get sloppy (usually 60-90 min). Tendons need months to adapt.
  • Skin management — file calluses smooth (don't let them tear). Use climbing balm at night.

📅 The 3-Week Plan

WEEK 1 — REFINE FOUNDATIONS

Goal: Perfect technique on 4s and 5s, build consistency, identify weaknesses.

Daily Session (~60 min):
  • 10 min — Easy traversing on 4a-4c (stay low, move sideways along the wall)
  • 20 min — Flash every 5a-5c in the gym. Focus on quiet feet (place feet precisely, no scraping)
  • 20 min — Attempt 6a problems. Read the route first (visualize moves before climbing)
  • 10 min — Stretching + cool down
💡 Quiet Feet Drill: Every time your foot makes noise on the wall, down-climb and restart. This builds precision faster than anything.
WEEK 2 — TECHNIQUE

Goal: Drill technique, start sending 6a-6b consistently.

Daily Session (~75 min):
  • 10 min — Warm-up traversing + 4a-5a problems
  • 15 min — Technique drill (rotate daily, see below)
  • 30 min — Project 6a-6b problems. 3-4 attempts per problem, then move on.
  • 10 min — Repeat a 5a-5b you've done, but try it with straight arms only
  • 10 min — Stretching (hip flexors, shoulders, forearms)
💡 The "3 before me" rule: Before attempting a problem, watch 3 other climbers on it. You'll learn beta (sequences) passively.

WEEK 3 — POWER & PROJECTS

Goal: Send 6b-6c consistently, start touching 7a projects.

Daily Session (~90 min):
  • 10 min — Warm up (4a-5b problems, traversing)
  • 10 min — Technique drill of the day
  • 40 min — Project time. Pick 2-3 problems at 6b-6c (or a 7a if feeling strong). Work moves individually, then link.
  • 15 min — Volume: repeat 5-6 problems at 5b-5c (build endurance + clean technique)
  • 15 min — Cool down, stretch, forearm rolling
💡 Projecting: Break hard problems into sections. Climb the top half, then the bottom half, then link. This is how strong climbers work.

🦴 Injury Prevention

⚠️ Your tendons are the bottleneck. Muscles adapt in weeks; tendons take months. This is the #1 reason beginners get injured.

  • Minimize crimping on 6s+ — favor open-hand grip (fingers curved, not bent at 90°)
  • Skip the campus board — seriously, don't touch it for at least 6 months
  • Listen to finger pain — sharp pain in finger pulleys = STOP immediately. Dull pump = normal.
  • Rest days: Even going daily, take at least 1 full rest day per week. Do yoga/stretching instead.
  • Antagonist exercises (2-3x/week): push-ups, reverse wrist curls, shoulder external rotation

🤲 Grip Types Cheat Sheet

Open Hand 👋

Fingers follow the natural curve. Safest grip. Use this by default.

Half Crimp ✊

Fingers bent ~90° at second knuckle, thumb NOT over fingers. More power, moderate risk.

Full Crimp 🔒

Thumb locks over index finger. Most power but highest injury risk. Avoid as beginner.

Pinch 🤏

Thumb opposes fingers. Trains a different set of muscles. Good variety.

🎯 Technique Tips

  • Read before you climb — stand back, trace the route with your eyes, plan your sequence
  • Straight arms = free energy — hang from your skeleton, not your muscles
  • Look at your feet — watch your toe hit the foothold. Don't just slap and hope.
  • Twist, don't pull — turn your body sideways to reach farther (drop-knee, backstepping)
  • Breathe — exhale on hard moves. Most beginners hold their breath.
  • Use momentum — static climbing is great for learning, but dynamic moves unlock harder grades
  • Hip positioning — your hips should be close to the wall and shifted toward the holding hand
  • Three points of contact — move one limb at a time until you're comfortable
  • Fall practice — early on, deliberately fall from safe heights. Reduces fear, improves commitment.

🏋️‍♂️ Powerlifting + Bouldering Program

The idea: Bouldering is pull-dominant. Powerlifting fills in everything it misses — pressing, legs, posterior chain. Together they build complete athletic strength.

⚠️ Golden rule: Never heavy deadlift the day before a hard bouldering session. Your grip and back need to be fresh for the wall.

🚫 Skip on Lift Days

  • Pull-ups / Rows — you're already pulling 3x/week on the wall. Adding more = overuse.
  • Heavy grip work — your fingers need recovery between boulder sessions.
  • Bicep curls — bouldering already hammers your biceps hard.
💡 If you boulder and lift on the same day, boulder FIRST. Grip and coordination need to be fresh for the wall.

📈 Progression

Use simple linear progression while you can:

  • Squat / Bench / DL / OHP: Add 2.5kg per session when you complete all sets. If you fail, repeat the same weight next time.
  • Accessories: When you hit the top of the rep range (e.g. 3×12 on dips), add weight next session.
  • Deload week: Every 4th week, drop to 60% of your working weights. Use this week for high-volume bouldering.

🍗 Recovery & Nutrition

  • Protein: 1.6-2.0g per kg bodyweight. Non-negotiable with this training volume.
  • Sleep: 7-9 hours. This is where gains happen. Both climbing and lifting suffer on bad sleep.
  • Finger recovery: If finger tendons feel sore (not pumped, but sore), skip the next boulder session. Tendon injuries take months to heal.
  • Creatine: 5g daily — helps with power output on both the wall and the platform.
  • Hydration: Chalk dries your skin, caffeine dehydrates — drink more water than you think.
