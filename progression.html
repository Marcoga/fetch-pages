🧗 The Progression Path

A roadmap from bouldering foundations to advanced projecting (4a – 7c)

4a – 4c

The Entry Level

Vertical walls with large "ladder" holds. Focus is on overcoming fear of heights and basic coordination.

Key to Master:

  • Center of Gravity: Keeping your weight over your feet.
  • Straight Arms: Learning to hang rather than pull.
  • Basic Footwork: Stepping with the toes, not the middle of the foot.
5a – 5c

The Foundation

Introduction of smaller holds (crimps/slopers) and slight overhangs. Requires specific body positions.

Key to Master:

  • Hip Positioning: Turning your hip toward the wall to increase reach.
  • Flags: Using your non-stepping leg as a counterweight for balance.
  • Quiet Feet: Placing feet precisely and silently (no scraping).
6a – 6c

The Intermediate Wall

Where most recreational climbers plateau. Holds get significantly smaller, and moves become more "dynamic" or "pumpy."

Key to Master:

  • Heel/Toe Hooks: Using your legs to pull your body into the wall.
  • Route Reading: Visualizing the entire "beta" (sequence) before leaving the ground.
  • Grip Strength: Transitioning from full-hand holds to finger-strength-dependent crimps.
  • Deadpoints: Moving dynamically to a hold at the apex of your momentum.
7a – 7c

The Advanced Tier

Elite-level movement. Tiny holds, massive overhangs, and highly complex sequences requiring extreme tension.

Key to Master:

  • Full Body Tension: Engaging core, toes, and fingers as one unit to stay on the wall.
  • Micro-Beta: Adjusting finger placement by millimeters to make a move possible.
  • Contact Strength: The ability to latch small holds instantly at high speed.
  • Projecting Mindset: Spending weeks or months on a single 5-move problem.

⏳ Estimated Progression Timeline

Current State: Beginner/Entry (5s & few 6a)

You have the baseline strength. You're likely "muscling" through 5s but getting stuck on 6s because they require better technique.

Goal 1: Consistent 6s (6a – 6b)

3–6 Months

Focus on volume of 5c's and repeating 6a's until they feel easy. Once you stop "re-adjusting" your hands and feet on every hold, you'll be here.

Goal 2: Intermediate Master (Most 6s, some 7a)

12–18 Months

This requires consistent training (2-3x/week). You'll need to develop specific finger strength and master "unintuitive" moves like drop-knees and high-steps.

Goal 3: The 7s Club (7a – 7c)

2–4 Years

To reach 7c, bouldering usually becomes a primary lifestyle focus. It involves dedicated fingerboard training, strict weight management (power-to-weight ratio), and high-level injury prevention.
